# Planning hierarchy

CanonLoom generates long-form plans from large units to small units, but each level has a different job. A lower-level plan may elaborate an approved upper-level decision; it may not silently rewrite it.

```text
Series / Project
    ↓
Volume
    ↓
Arc / Part
    ↓
Chapter Contract
    ↓
Beat Sheet
    ↓
Scene
    ↓
Prose
```

## Series / project level

Answers:

- What is the durable reader promise?
- What kind of experience should the whole work deliver?
- What is the central question or pressure?
- Which boundaries must remain stable?
- What can change across volumes?

Outputs: intent, reader promise, project boundaries, long-range state dimensions, and evaluation policy.

## Volume level

Answers:

- What pressure dominates this volume?
- What changes between the opening and closing state?
- Which character, relationship, institution, resource, or rule is transformed?
- What is the volume's main engine and counter-engine?
- What must remain unresolved for the next volume?

Outputs: volume promise, opening state, closing state, major turns, resource and relationship trajectories, open loops, and handoff.

## Arc / part level

Answers:

- What local problem makes the volume pressure concrete?
- Which decisions accumulate across this block?
- What is the escalation pattern?
- Where are the recovery, relationship, and consequence beats?

Outputs: arc goal, chapter range, state transitions, key decisions, and a short-cycle review plan.

## Chapter contract level

Answers:

- What is the current reader question?
- Who must choose, under what pressure?
- What information is available to each relevant character?
- What cannot be achieved simultaneously?
- What visible cost follows the choice?
- What changes irreversibly at the end?

Outputs: chapter contract, evidence references, forbidden changes, open loops, and exit state.

## Beat level

Every scene or beat should identify:

```text
goal → resistance / information gap → observable action
→ immediate result → changed state
```

A beat is an execution unit, not a miniature summary. It must leave the next beat with a changed situation.

## Downward and upward control

```text
Downward: promise → pressure → decision → action → prose
Upward:   prose → observed change → review evidence → state delta
```

If a lower-level idea requires a new fact or a change to an upper-level decision, it becomes a candidate decision and stops automatic generation. The author can accept it, revise the plan, or reject it.

## Planning gates

| Gate | Question | Failure response |
|---|---|---|
| P0 intent | Is the desired reading experience explicit? | clarify intent |
| P1 volume | Does the volume have a start state, pressure, and end state? | redesign volume |
| P2 arc | Do chapters accumulate decisions rather than repeat tasks? | change arc engine |
| P3 contract | Is there a current question, choice, cost, and consequence? | block drafting |
| P4 beats | Does every beat change the situation? | repair beat sheet |
| P5 prose | Does the draft execute the approved path within viewpoint and canon? | revise or escalate |
